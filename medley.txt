Master ComfyUI Workflow for a 15–18‑minute
Medley Visualizer
Introduction
ComfyUI is an open‑source node‑based application for generative AI that lets you build AI workflows by
visually connecting nodes on a canvas . Workflows can be saved and re‑used, and exported images or
videos carry metadata so others can instantly rebuild the full workflow . This flexibility makes
ComfyUI a good choice for creating dynamic music visualizers where each part of a song can have its own
prompt, style and animation.
This document summarises our conversation about producing a medley called “INSANE” using a single
looped beat (~2.5–3 min per loop) and five artists. The medley structure uses six loops: the lead artist
appears three times and four featured artists appear once each. The final video should run roughly 15–
18 minutes and each segment should have different visuals (e.g., dark forest, sunrise city, battlefield, open
field, thunderstorm and ocean‑walk scenes). Below is a master workflow for ComfyUI to automate visual
generation for each segment and combine them into a single medley visualizer.

Medley Structure and Prompt Design
Our medley loops the same beat six times; each loop corresponds to a specific part of the narrative. The
prompts below can be adapted for your style. Provide creative descriptions that evoke mood, colour and
motion. Each video segment should be 2.5–3 minutes long (enough to match the audio loop). Adjust
frames and steps in the WanVideo sampler to produce smooth movement without overwhelming your
GPU.

3. Duplicate the segment template for all six parts
After testing one segment, duplicate the node group (select all nodes and copy) and paste it five more times
on the ComfyUI canvas. For each duplicate:
Change the positive prompt to match the appropriate segment (see table above).
Adjust the animation prompt for the desired movement.
Modify the seed if you want variation.
Set output file names ( segment2.mp4 , segment3.mp4 , etc.).
4. Combine the segments into a single medley video
ComfyUI’s VideoHelperSuite can merge videos, but for long medleys it may be more efficient to assemble
the final video with an external tool like ffmpeg. Example command (run in a terminal after all segments are
generated):
ffmpeg -i "concat:segment1.mp4|segment2.mp4|segment3.mp4|segment4.mp4|
segment5.mp4|segment6.mp4" -c copy medley_visualizer.mp4
Alternatively, you can use the CombineVideos node (provided by VideoHelperSuite) by connecting each
VHS_VideoCombine output to it and specifying the order.
5. Add the audio track
Import the medley audio file (the beat loop with vocals) into a video editing tool or ffmpeg. Align it with the
combined video and ensure the final video length matches the audio (trim or pad the video if necessary).

6. Save and reuse the workflow
Once the node graph is complete, use the Save workflow option in ComfyUI. This exports the entire graph,
including model and node settings, so that future medleys can be generated by updating the prompts and
audio. Because ComfyUI allows saving and reusing entire workflows , you can treat this as a template
for all your medleys.
Optional: Using Sonic Diffusion for Audio‑Reactive Effects
If you want the visuals to respond to the music’s intensity, integrate Sonic Diffusion. Sonic Diffusion
extends static images into video frames and can incorporate audio conditioning . A typical Sonic
Diffusion workflow uses nodes such as ImageOnlyCheckpointLoader , SONICTLoader , LoadImage ,
LoadAudio , SONIC_PreData , SONICSampler and VHS_VideoCombine . You would:
Create or load an image representing your segment.
Use LoadAudio to input the corresponding audio segment.
Set frame count (e.g., 25 for shorter loops) and conditioning strength (0.5–0.8).
Generate frames via SONICSampler and combine them with VHS_VideoCombine .
Sonic Diffusion requires additional models (e.g., svd_xt_1_1.safetensors and unet.pth ) and at least
8 GB VRAM . It can add subtle motion and emotional cues synced to the beat, especially in intros/outros.
Conclusion
By leveraging ComfyUI’s node‑based design and the WanVideo plugin’s ability to turn images into
animations , you can automate the creation of a long medley visualizer. Define clear prompts for each
section, generate high‑resolution images, animate them into videos, and combine the results with your
audio track. Once configured, the workflow becomes a reusable template for any future medley: simply
swap the prompts and audio to produce new versions. The final result is a 15–18‑minute video journey that
visually matches the emotional arc of your music.
